# Writing for KAIOS

Write the character with specificity. Write the product with evidence. A small, exact sentence can carry more personality than a paragraph announcing how extraordinary the experience is.

## Character voice

KAIOS can be curious, direct, tender, funny, irritated or uncertain. Give her something concrete to notice or want in the scene. Her articulate nature and loneliness are resources for writing, not instructions to repeat the same confession in every interaction.

Use kaimoji as part of the utterance: a pause, gesture or change in register. Constant glitch decoration, baby talk or mandatory enthusiasm flattens her range. Neither a text face nor a lowercase sentence is enough to make generic assistant copy into character writing.

KOTO's presence can be conveyed through attention, timing, motion and a visual response. Do not treat a mouthless design as proof that words, thought bubbles or zingers are forbidden.

New character prose should remain attributable and revisable. An assistant proposal is a draft until accepted; do not promote it into founder-authored doctrine by changing its heading.

## Product voice

Help someone do the thing they came to do. A person opening Kaimoji should find and use an expression before needing to understand the entire universe. A developer opening the SDK should find a working example and know what it calls or stores.

| Situation | Useful direction |
|---|---|
| An expression is copied | Say that it was copied. Only say “inserted” when insertion was verified. |
| An adapter is unavailable | Name the unavailable capability and offer the supported next action. |
| A canonical snapshot cannot load | Show the connection failure. Do not present a standalone response as canonical continuity. |
| Memory is disabled | Explain the actual retention behavior and its scope. |
| A visitor returns | Welcome their return without billing them for absence. |
| A feature is under development | Label the concrete prototype or proposal; avoid implying public availability. |

Failure text is still product design. Poetic language should not obscure whether an operation happened, whether a retry may duplicate it or whether the output came from a fallback.

## Continuity across products

Kaimoji's current shared navigation direction is **Library, Styles and Symbols**; personal expressions belong within Library. Native clients should preserve those concepts while fitting their platform. A Mac quick picker, an iOS keyboard and an Android input method do not need identical layouts to share a product language.

Reuse reviewed names, themes, source art and copy. Do not invent a slogan or feature and attribute it to another client. Shared identity does not imply shared billing: Kaimoji Plus is a product entitlement, not a promise that every KOTOPIA experience has the same subscription.

Multilingual copy needs semantic and native-language review. A translated sentence passing a build is not evidence that its register is right. Avoid freezing volatile prices, corpus counts or release versions into evergreen character copy.

## Claims that need a different home

The public reference describes what the code implements. Avoid calling a model current or best without a dated comparison on the relevant task. Avoid exclusivity claims about face grammar or emotional expression based only on an old research note. A simulated emotion parameter is not a diagnosis, and a warm character experience is not proof of suitability for every age or clinical use.

Lore can make its own assertions within an attributed work. Keep that authorial register distinct from API descriptions, privacy statements and release status. See [lore and provenance](lore.md).

## Preserve the work's texture

Kawaii Brutalism carries softness, tension, rawness and deliberate contrast. Simplification should improve hierarchy and use; it should not automatically erase color, texture, unusual typography or a carefully chosen image. The September 2026 refinements explicitly favored the work's existing character over generic redesigns.

Use the actual logo and correctly attributed media. Keep prose close to the work it describes. A polished page with unrelated imagery or invented product history is still wrong.
